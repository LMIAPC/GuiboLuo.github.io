01 / RESEARCH

Research directions that connect

Our work spans medical image analysis, physiological and behavioral modeling, and the training and development of large AI models. These four directions are closely connected.

01Medical Imaging AI

Intelligent Medical Image Analysis

We develop methods for lesion detection and segmentation, disease recognition, quantitative analysis, and risk assessment using multi-center and multimodal medical imaging data, with a focus on generalization across heterogeneous clinical environments and real-world applicability.

02Physiological & Behavioral Intelligence

EEG and Multimodal Physiological-Behavioral Modeling

We investigate temporal representation learning, multimodal fusion, human-state estimation, and behavioral understanding by integrating EEG, ECG, eye-tracking, video, and other physiological and behavioral signals, with an emphasis on generalization across individuals and real-world settings.

03Multimodal Foundation Models

Multimodal Foundation Models & Efficient Training and Reasoning

We explore unified representations and cross-modal alignment across vision, language, and physiological signals, alongside foundation model pretraining, reinforcement learning-based post-training, knowledge distillation, advanced reasoning, and inference optimization to improve model capabilities and computational efficiency.

04Trustworthy Collaborative Learning

Federated Learning & Privacy-Preserving AI

We study personalized federated learning, privacy-preserving methods, communication-efficient collaboration, knowledge transfer, and federated foundation models, addressing data heterogeneity, privacy constraints, and decentralized learning to enable trustworthy and efficient AI across institutions and distributed environments.

We welcome ideas that cross these boundaries, especially when they address a clear research question and can be tested rigorously.

Background, responsibilities & growth

Select a role to see what we value and the kind of work you could pursue. Research milestones are aspirations, not universal admission or graduation requirements.

01Postdoctoral ResearchersResearch leadership · Collaboration · Mentorship

Who may be a good fit

  • A PhD in computer science, artificial intelligence, electrical or electronic engineering, biomedical engineering, clinical medicine, or a related field, with research experience relevant to our work.
  • Evidence that you can identify important questions, design convincing experiments, interpret results, and lead scientific writing.
  • Strong representative research contributions; experience collaborating across disciplines is welcome.

Research scope & development

  • Lead research projects aligned with the group’s interests while developing a distinct scholarly identity.
  • Take part in research proposals and collaborations, and contribute to mentoring junior researchers.
  • Work toward a small body of substantial contributions—for example, two or three representative research outputs over a suitable appointment period—rather than optimizing for publication count.
Research expectations

The research plan will be developed together, taking account of appointment length, project maturity, and your longer-term career goals.

POSTDOCTORAL COMPENSATIONRMB 400,000–600,000 total annual compensation (for eligible outstanding candidates; actual compensation and its components depend on qualifications, appointment category, and host-institution policy).
02PhD StudentsResearch depth · Original thinking · Scientific independence

Who may be a good fit

  • A sound foundation in mathematics, programming, and machine learning, with a willingness to keep learning.
  • Intellectual curiosity, initiative, and the resilience to pursue a difficult problem over time.
  • Previous papers or projects are useful, but we place greater weight on your specific contributions, reasoning, and research potential.

Research scope & development

  • Build a coherent thesis direction around related problems, rather than a collection of disconnected projects.
  • As a flexible aspiration, develop a connected body of roughly three to five strong papers during the PhD; originality, rigor, and impact matter more than the number.
  • Progressively take ownership of research design, experimentation, writing, collaboration, and mentoring of junior students.
Research expectations

These milestones are adjusted to your preparation, progress, and interests; paper counts are not a standalone measure of success or a statement of degree requirements.

03Master’s StudentsMethod development · Reproducible systems · Practical experience

Who may be a good fit

  • Some preparation in mathematics, programming, or AI, together with an interest in developing these skills.
  • Reliability, a willingness to learn, and genuine interest in scientific research or research engineering.

Research scope & development

  • Complete a reasonably self-contained research or research-engineering project, independently or as part of a team.
  • Produce a verifiable result, which might be a paper, a reusable training or evaluation framework, a model, or a working research prototype.
  • Learn how to design experiments, reproduce findings, document work, and communicate results clearly.
How we assess progress

Rigorous research and well-executed research engineering are both valued. A paper is not the only meaningful outcome.

04Undergraduate Research InternsResearch foundations · Curiosity · Remote options by arrangement

Who may be a good fit

  • Primarily second- and third-year undergraduates with basic programming skills and genuine curiosity about research.
  • An initial commitment of at least three months is recommended; timing can be discussed around coursework and other commitments.
  • No publications are expected. We value questions, consistent communication, and honest reporting of results.

Research scope & development

  • Understand and reproduce a representative research method or experiment.
  • Carry out a reproducible benchmark, robustness assessment, or failure-case study.
  • Write a short, evidence-based account of what worked, what did not, and what you would investigate next.
Research outcomes

A publication is neither required nor promised. Longer-term involvement may be possible depending on research fit, supervision capacity, and applicable policies.

Openings, funding, compensation, admissions eligibility, degree arrangements, and any programme-specific language requirements are subject to the current policies of the relevant host institution and programme. International applicants should consult official admission or appointment information.

03 / HOW WE WORK

A culture of curiosity and rigor

Strong research outcomes matter, but so do the questions we ask, the evidence we gather, and how we work with others.

01

Curiosity

Ask why an approach works, where it breaks down, and what remains unexplained.

02

Independent thinking

Question assumptions and develop ideas that can be tested, rather than stopping at reproduced results.

03

Scientific rigor

Value reproducibility, sound evaluation, negative results, and clear acknowledgment of limitations.

04

Ownership

Take initiative, communicate clearly, and be accountable for your methods, claims, and collaborations.
